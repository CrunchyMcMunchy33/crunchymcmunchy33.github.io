The Beginning of Feng

From Local Open Mics to a Distinct Voice

Feng started performing at local open mic nights and DIY venues, carving a niche with a raw, poetic lyricism and minimalist production. Early tracks were self-released and often recorded in one-take sessions, capturing an immediacy that resonated with early listeners. Feng's style blended spoken-word intimacy with spare beats, creating a sense of urgency and authenticity that quickly made him a standout in grassroots scenes.

Learning on the Road

His early career was shaped by countless small shows and collaborations with other local artists. Touring small circuits and playing late-night spots taught him how to read a crowd and refine his delivery. These experiences were as much education as practice—each gig provided lessons in pacing, audience interaction, and how to translate studio work into compelling live moments.

DIY Recording and Immediate Feedback

Feng's early recordings were deliberately low-fi, prioritizing emotional honesty over sonic polish. He used that aesthetic as a creative choice, not a limitation—releasing music quickly and letting audiences respond before overworking a song. This approach built a direct feedback loop that informed later projects and helped develop a loyal base of listeners who appreciated directness and vulnerability in his music.

Foundations for a Unique Career

Those formative years established Feng's core strengths: lyrical candor, a keen sense for atmosphere, and an ability to build connection in intimate performance settings. They set the foundation for the artistic risks and broader recognition that would come later.
